Theory — How Plasma Cutting Works

Plasma cutting is TIG welding with compressed air doing the cutting.

ComponentTIG WelderPlasma Cutter
Arc creationHF start or touch startHF pilot arc
GasArgon/helium (shielding)Compressed air (cutting medium)
ElectrodeTungsten (non-consumable)Hafnium (consumed over time)
NozzleOpen cup (gas dispersal)Constricted hole (arc concentration)
PurposeWeld metal togetherCut metal apart
Air speedLow flow60-80 PSI high velocity

Key insight: The nozzle constriction is what makes plasma. Without it, you just get a hot flame. The HF board is the same in both TIG and plasma — only the torch design differs.

Surgical Wiring — Exact Connections

Connection 1 — Main Power Input

220V AC live wire → Transformer primary. Use 2.5mm minimum wire. Add 30 amp fuse in series for safety.

Connection 2 — Transformer to Rectifier

Transformer secondary (50-80V AC) → Bridge rectifier input. Use 6mm minimum wire — carries full cutting current.

Connection 3 — Rectifier to Plasma Board

Rectifier POSITIVE → Plasma board DC+ input. Rectifier NEGATIVE → Ground clamp. Add 50-100A fuse.

Connection 4 — HF Board Wiring

HF board power from DC bus. HF output (15KV) → torch electrode. USE SILICONE INSULATED WIRE for high voltage.

Connection 5 — Air Solenoid Wiring

Solenoid wires → plasma board relay output. Relay switches air ON when trigger pressed.

Connection 6 — Torch Connection

Three wires: Electrode → HF output + DC positive. Nozzle → pilot arc circuit. Trigger → board trigger input.

Connection 7 — Ground Clamp

DC negative → Work piece ground. Use 10mm minimum cable — heaviest in system. Clean contact surface before clamping.

The 6-Step Power Joint

1. Clean — isopropyl alcohol + sandpaper
2. Tin — apply solder to wire and pad separately
3. Heat — touch both with iron for 5-8 seconds
4. Flow — solder must penetrate wire strands
5. Cool — DO NOT MOVE for 10 seconds
6. Verify — multimeter continuity test
